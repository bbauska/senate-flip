# Senate seats flipped 2026

Seven Senate seats. At least 24 House districts. Five governorships. Twelve state legislative chambers. 
By Wednesday afternoon, that’s how far Democrats’ historic midterm rout had reached.

Republicans, meanwhile, had yet to flip a single Democratic-held congressional seat, governorship or 
state legislative chamber, according to Associated Press data. Their remaining opportunities for 
congressional pickups are largely confined to two newly gerrymandered House districts that weren’t 
even supposed to be competitive in the first place.

The lopsided results underscored the breadth of the GOP’s shellacking, with Democrats breaking 
decades of Republican dominance in reliably red states and reshaping the balance of power from 
Washington to state capitols.

As of late Wednesday, nine House seats remained uncalled by the AP along with two Senate seats 
and two governors races. The one seat Republicans may have a chance of flipping that hasn’t been 
redistricted ahead of this election is Maine’s 2nd District, FiftyPlusOne head of research Mary 
Radcliffe said by email.

In Kansas, voters elected their first Democratic U.S. senator since 1932, ending a 94-year losing 
streak for the party. In Texas, Democrats toppled three-term Republican Gov. Greg Abbott and 
captured a Senate seat that had eluded the party since 1988. Republican-held Senate seats also 
fell in Iowa, South Carolina and Ohio, along with seats in Maine and North Carolina.

The same wave swept through governors’ mansions. Democrats scored upsets in Georgia, Iowa, Ohio 
nd Nevada — all states President Donald Trump won in 2024 — giving the party a majority of the 
nation’s governorships for the first time since 2010.

Upheaval even reached state capitols, where Republican legislative majorities that had endured 
for decades fell like dominoes.

Democrats seized control of the Texas House for the first time in more than two decades, flipped 
both chambers of the Wisconsin Legislature and wrested the North Carolina House from Republicans 
after 16 years in the minority.

In Pennsylvania, Democrats captured the state Senate for the first time since before 1994, 
giving the party complete control of state government. They also reclaimed lower chambers in 
Michigan and Minnesota, restoring full Democratic control in both states. In Arizona, 
unofficial results pointed toward Democrats taking both legislative chambers for the first 
time in roughly 60 years.

Even where Republicans held on to legislative majorities, Democrats stripped away veto-proof 
supermajorities in Florida, Indiana, Kansas and Missouri. Not since the aftermath of Watergate 
in 1974 had the GOP faced a statehouse reckoning of this magnitude, according to the Democratic 
Legislative Campaign Committee.

The scale of the losses is all the more remarkable given how aggressively Republicans had tried 
to protect their power. At Trump’s urging, GOP lawmakers spent months pursuing an extraordinary 
mid-decade redistricting campaign, redrawing congressional boundaries in Texas, Florida and North 
Carolina to tilt the electoral map in their favor.

Yet the gerrymandering push did little to blunt the Democratic wave. And the damage might not be 
over. Republicans were still fighting to hold Senate seats in Alaska and Mississippi, with several 
House contests also yet to be called Wednesday.

— Kevin Breuninger contributed to this report.
